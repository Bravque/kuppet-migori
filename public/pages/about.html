About KUPPET Migori

Three decades of unwavering commitment to the rights, welfare, and professional excellence of post-primary education teachers in Migori County.

Who We Are

Our Purpose & Principles

Our Vision

To be a model branch of excellence in Advocacy, Leadership and offering Service with Distinction.

Our Mission

To be a leading branch in effectively representing, protecting and advancing the professional, economic, and social interests of members through transparent governance, institutional strengthening, capacity development and innovative service delivery to secondary teachers in Migori.

Our Commitment

We are committed to ensuring every teacher in Migori County works with dignity, receives fair compensation, and has access to professional growth opportunities and welfare support.

Guiding Principles

Our Core Values

Advocacy

We are unapologetic in our advocacy for better pay, safer working environments, and policy reforms that elevate the teaching profession.

Welfare

We are driven by a genuine commitment to serve our members — every program, campaign, and initiative is designed around the teacher's needs.

Accountability

Our leaders are accountable to members. We maintain transparent financial management and regular reporting on all union activities and resources.

Integrity

We uphold the highest standards of honesty, transparency, and accountability in all our dealings with members and stakeholders.

Inclusivity

We celebrate diversity and ensure every member — regardless of gender, age, or sub-county — has equal voice and representation in KUPPET Migori.

Professionalism

We champion continuous professional development and hold our members to the highest standards of educational excellence and ethical conduct.

Justice

We advocate for fair treatment, equal opportunity, and equitable working conditions for every teacher, regardless of gender, region, or background.

Solidarity

We believe in the strength of collective action. Together, KUPPET members speak with one powerful voice that commands attention and respect.

Our Journey

Branch History

From humble beginnings to becoming the strongest teachers' voice in Migori County.

The idea of forming a union to champion the interests of post-primary teachers — those in secondary schools, teacher training colleges and polytechnics — was first pronounced by Rashid Kawewa in 1998. At the time, the interests of the post-primary teacher were not well canvassed within KNUT, whose primary-school section practised a tyranny of numbers: with the majority of members being primary-school teachers, it was never easy for a secondary or college teacher to win a seat, and the primary section dictated the line-up in every election. The move to form a separate union gained momentum after the death of the all-powerful KNUT Secretary General, Mr. Ambrose Adongo.

One day in October 2001, a meeting was held at Gilly Hotel, attended by Mr. Tom Chariga, Wanyonyi Buteyo, Akelo Misori, Charles Ooko, Odunga Nyamusi, Lazarus Abila, Ogutu Ode P.C., Mr. Yogo, Tobias Onyango and Oscar Ayaga, among others. The meeting was chaired by Mr. Wanyonyi Buteyo, with Mr. Tom Chariga taking the minutes. An interim office was established: Executive Secretary Akelo M.T. Misori, Assistant E.S. Mr. Yogo, Chairman Mr. Ogutu Ode P.C., Vice Chairman Odunga Nyamusi and Treasurer Tobias Onyango. When Mr. Yogo passed on, Mr. Charles Ooko was elected unanimously by the interim officials to replace him.

An office was established at Kadika Plaza in Migori town, with the officials paying rent in a monthly rotational manner. Akelo, Ogutu, Ooko and Nyamusi were the most active branch officials, each contributing Kshs. 3,000 per month — there were no remittances from the TSC at the time. The officials embarked on popularising KUPPET as the union with the best advocacy policies for teachers, in professional gatherings and even during burial ceremonies. Resistance was met from government officials and KNUT, and KUPPET was blackmailed by rivals who called it “Kupe” and “puppet”. From Migori, the team went on to Kuria, where another branch was established under Executive Secretary Mr. Murimi and Chairman Mr. Majina.

In the run-up to the 2011 elections, the two branches of Migori and Kuria agreed to merge, forming the current Migori County branch — as was the national trend to make KUPPET stronger at the grassroots. The first merger officials were Chairman Mr. Kennedy Makasembo, Executive Secretary Mr. John Kennedy Obonyo and Treasurer Mr. Omukereri, alongside Hilda Ghati (Assistant E.S.), Paul Migwambo (Vice Chairman) and Angela Odero (Assistant Treasurer), among others. This is the team that midwifed the county structure of KUPPET, on whose foundation the branch currently stands.

1998

The Idea is Born

Rashid Kawewa first pronounces the idea of a union for post-primary teachers, whose interests were not well canvassed within KNUT. The move gains momentum after the death of KNUT Secretary General Ambrose Adongo.

2001

Migori Branch Formed

A founding meeting at Gilly Hotel in October 2001, chaired by Wanyonyi Buteyo, establishes the interim office: E.S. Akelo M.T. Misori, Chairman Ogutu Ode P.C., Vice Chairman Odunga Nyamusi and Treasurer Tobias Onyango.

2001

First Office at Kadika Plaza

An office opens at Kadika Plaza, Migori town. With no TSC remittances yet, active officials Akelo, Ogutu, Ooko and Nyamusi each pay Kshs. 3,000 monthly rent, popularising KUPPET despite resistance from government and KNUT.

2000s

Kuria Branch Established

From Migori, the team extends to Kuria, where a branch is established under Executive Secretary Mr. Murimi and Chairman Mr. Majina.

2011

Migori County Branch Merger

Ahead of the 2011 elections, the Migori and Kuria branches merge to form the current Migori County branch, led by Chairman Kennedy Makasembo, E.S. John Kennedy Obonyo and Treasurer Mr. Omukereri — the team that midwifed the county structure.

Today

A Strong Grassroots Voice

Built on that foundation, KUPPET Migori now represents post-primary teachers across all twelve sub-counties of Migori County, advancing their professional, economic and social interests.
